---
name: scope-change
description: Decomposition gate of the chain and its re-decomposition point. Use when a change must be structured — the entry decomposition before specification, a coupling exposed mid-cycle by audit-change/reconcile-change (a re-decomposition), or a gate fix that expands the change. Profiles every touched surface (code/docs/tests/configs), builds the multi-surface DSM, and issues the verdict computed from the profile and the project's calibration (single-cycle | program with sequencing | coupled block land-together or merge); the author is stopped at three named points — a red gate (the launch boundary), a fired deferred option's trigger, and a verdict path that cannot land: a calibration that cannot determine the change's shape, one that determines it void, or a collapse that cannot pass its checklist. Output — the charter.

# scope-change

Terminology — [`../../reference/glossary.md`](../../reference/glossary.md) (the term canon; an entry naming a carrier skill defines that mechanism at the carrier).

## Role in the chain

`scope-change → specify-change → audit-change → realize-change → verify-change → reconcile-change`

The string is the forward path; the chain also returns — `blocked` from `audit-change` to `specify-change`, and a coupling exposed mid-cycle to a `re-decomposition` here.

- **Entry:** a change request (input), or a `re-decomposition` return — a coupling exposed mid-cycle by `audit-change` (the `amplitude control bounds` stop asks "re-decompose?") or by `reconcile-change`, or a gate fix that expands the change; the decision to return is the author's.
- **Pre-condition:** three mechanical steps in order, before profiling — the orchestrator runs each check and reads the status only (the checks' internals are not the orchestrator's business). A self-change — the corpus's own — runs the same three steps with the corpus standing in for the project: its own checks stand for the gates (the corpus's check suite — the check scripts of its `tools/` and its CI, run locally), its `docs/change-calibration.md` is the calibration, and that file's presence stands for the adoption flag (step 1 checks the file, not a flag):
  1. **Calibration (the adoption flag):** `tools/check-calibration <the project's directory in the operational layer — `projects/<id>/` in the overlay, `init-change`'s outputs' home>` — exit 0 = the adoption flag is present (the project's calibration was completed by `init-change`); exit 2 = the flag is absent — the project is not initialized, `init-change` first: the severity thresholds and necessity filter come from it (the gates themselves live in the project's `green-baseline.sh`, its artifact too); any other exit is a usage error or the check's own failure, not a non-adoption — the launch stops, the invocation corrected or the check repaired at its owner (the corpus ships it; a check defect routes to `reconcile-change`), and the launch resumes when the check runs clean.
  2. **Baseline tree:** resolved at launch by `tools/baseline-tree.sh` from the operator's launch directive (a worktree unconditionally; `branch:<name>`; `commit:<sha>`; as-is — the current state accepted into the change's base), or the chain default when no directive: a dirty tree isolates into a worktree from HEAD; a clean tree works on HEAD. A re-decomposition launch continues the stopped cycle's tree — the default there is the cycle's own working state (its worktree when it ran in one, its checkout as-is otherwise), its in-flight work included (the profiled facts stand on that tree, and `product_root` names it); the operator's directive overrides — a directive that moves the launch off the cycle's tree re-opens the profile (the standing facts stand for the parts whose content the two trees hold identically; the parts new to the tree or modified by the in-flight work are profiled like any other, their runs pinning their counts). A failed resolution — an unknown directive, an unresolvable start point — stops the launch for the operator: the directive is corrected and the launch re-runs its pre-condition.
  3. **Gates (green-baseline):** the orchestrator invokes the project's `green-baseline.sh` (lives in the project's operational layer, next to the adoption flag — `calibration.flag`, step 1's probe; exit 0 = green, non-zero = not green, stdout carries per-gate results) and reads the verdict. Any non-zero outcome → the authoring point: investigate and decide — fix the product (a prerequisite-change BEFORE this change — this launch stops and re-launches after it), revise the script (the script revision routes to `reconcile-change`, and this change re-launches after it), or accept the state as conditionally green. The gate outcome is the orchestrator's to carry: on a conditionally-green acceptance the orchestrator passes the author's decision and its grounds to the Profiler for recording in the charter. A change starts on a green gate — or on the author's conditionally-green acceptance, recorded in the charter's `gate_decision` (the prerequisite-change records its gate decision likewise: the decision that routed the red to fix-the-product is that record — the launch itself starts on the red it exists to fix).
  The gates run in the resolved baseline tree; where a gate runs in a container, it mounts the resolved tree by absolute path; the charter carries `product_root` of the resolved tree. The steps run at every launch, a re-decomposition included — the gates over the launch's resolved tree; a red there may be the stopped cycle's own in-flight work, and the disposition menu is read with that knowledge: the authoring point still asks, and the likely disposition for work red-pending by design is the conditionally-green acceptance, the author's decision recording it with its grounds.
- **Output:** the charter — the `decomposition:` block per the mandate's Output contract (the field list — the mandate's; the `anchor_verification` pin included). The charter is done when it parses under the mandate's parse rule (glossary: `output contract`).

## What it does

1. **Profile** — surfaces touched: code, docs, tests, configs (import graph, grep). size, coupling, risk, DDD-tier, blast-radius. Before any profiled reading, at the launch, the Profiler runs the registry interface — `tools/registry-index.sh` check → expiry → scope (glossary: `deferred-option`; the registry is reached only through its operations; the reading scope and the expired-item semantics — the mandate) — and matches the emitted matching scope against the launch's input facts; a fired trigger stops the run with the authoring question "execute the option?", never an auto-gate. The Profiler determines the **fitness-authority** (which checker is authoritative — a tool or human) and sets the **process mode**: `accumulate-then-verify` for tool-authoritative changes, `describe-then-apply` otherwise (glossary: `fitness-authority determines process-depth`, `accumulate-then-verify (tool-authoritative changes)`). **Formation-brief (R1, glossary: `spike`):** the orchestrator evaluates the trigger at launch from the launch's input data alone (the change request's breadth, or the re-decomposition's scope) — the pre-request code reading it would take to understand the change spans ≥2 thematic blocks or ~100k input (a retro estimate, made without reading) → a formation spike ≤40k input-equivalent, dispatched by the orchestrator before the Profiler's launch (a fresh subagent — ≠ profiler ≠ specifier ≠ verifier ≠ realizer: the claims' later consumers must not be their authors); output — the formation brief (the claim-list plus a topic TOC, `claim-list.md` in the cycle workspace — `scope-change-<slug>`, the slug of the launch), passed to the Profiler as an input option. **Knowledge-gap registry (R2):** the Profiler emits knowledge gaps after profiling (the threshold and routing — the mandate); a firing gap → a mid-launch spike, dispatched by the orchestrator against the Profiler's halt. A dispatched spike's return is governed by the two-branch completion rule (glossary: `dispatch-completion`): completion = the named product artifact (the claim-list — the mandate's declared form; the formation brief — the form its dispatch brief declares) parses; a product-less return is incomplete — the same agent completes it.
2. **Multi-surface DSM** — the dependency matrix over the parts of the change (rows = columns = the profiled files/artifacts grouped by the work they carry; a cell = a dependency between two parts, tagged by surface) **across all touched surfaces**, not code alone; a set of parts coupled on some surface is a `coupled block`; a block whose coupling lives on a non-code surface — the parts code-disjoint — is a `surface conflict`, recorded in `surface_conflict` (see glossary: `multi-surface DSM`).
3. **Verdict** — three granularity axes, independent decisions (glossary: `decomposition-verdict`; the charter's `verdict` token — `single_cycle | program | coupled_block`): **work-unit** (units of work/specification), **verify-unit** (units of verification), **land-unit** (units of merge). Deriving one axis from another (work from land semantics) is an antipattern.
   - **single-cycle** → severity-mode (Tiny/Small/Standard/Deep — thresholds in the project's calibration; every allocated cycle of any verdict carries its own severity — the mandate).
   - **program** → Parnas-seams + DSM-topology (independent / sequential / coupled-blocks) + sequencing + integration points.
   - a `coupled block` — on a code surface or a `surface conflict` (code-disjoint; coupled on a doc/test/config surface) — lands, the glossary's resolutions extended to the code-surface block: `land-together` (separate cycles, merged as a unit — **only if every cycle is green-committable**) or `merge` (if intermediate red cycles cannot be committed — e.g. project policy "no commit at red gate"; then one cycle, commit when all green).
   The verdict names the whole change: `coupled_block` when the profiled parts form one coupled block — on any surface — and nothing independent; several coupled blocks, or independent cycles alongside a coupled block, stay a `program`, each block landing by its resolution inside the sequencing (a surface conflict is recorded in `surface_conflict`).
   Any collapse of work granularity is issued through a recorded per-item pass of the Collapse-checklist (the Profiler's mandate: [`assets/decomposer-charter.md`](assets/decomposer-charter.md)).
4. **Necessity filter (do/don't-do)** — after the verdict, every cycle is filtered against the project's calibration (`init-change`'s artifact — `docs/change-calibration.md` in the project, its completion gated at launch by the adoption flag (the pre-condition); absent until adoption) — glossary: `necessity filter`: **DO** / **DEFER** (a record in the deferred-options registry (glossary: `deferred-option`) — the schema, the index re-mint and the index discipline — the mandate) / **DROP** (rationale in the charter, bypassing the registry). A program of N cycles → some may turn out speculative/over-engineering (DROP — out of the work for good), others a long-term investment on an unstable stage (DEFER — kept live in the registry, outside this change's work). Methodology-derived, not a user menu.
5. **Charter** — the `decomposition:` block per the mandate's Output contract (the field list lives there), assembled from the products of steps 1–4 — the `registry` field carrying the knowledge gaps (per the mandate), the anchor pass's dead claims with their reasons, and, on a voided change, the written items' pending retirement.

## Escalation — the calibration's insufficiency

The verdict is computed from the profile and the project's calibration (`init-change`'s artifact): severity from its thresholds, the coupled-block resolution from its commit/deploy policy, the scope boundaries from its dimension of boundaries. The standard path does not stop for the author. The halt fires on the Profiler's conclusion, in one of three forms: **the calibration cannot determine the change's shape** (it is silent for the case, ambiguous between outcomes, or contradicted by the profiled facts); **the calibration, applied honestly, voids the change** — the necessity filter excluding every allocated cycle, the calibration's verdict against the change itself; or **a collapse that cannot pass its checklist** — a second violation on the re-derived shape, the shape cannot be passed. The escalation carries the verdict-path's findings — on the insufficiency form, the gap (its type, what remains undetermined); on the void form, the exclusions and their grounds; on the unpassable-shape form, the violations and their justifications — the analysis and the Profiler's recommendation in all; the process stops and waits; the run proceeds only on the author's answer, and the answer routes what it decides — an insufficiency's calibration update, or a void's retirements — through `reconcile-change`; a re-scoped shape is the launch's own in-frame revision (the mandate's revision clause).

## Cycle

1. File exchange: the cycle workspace.
2. The orchestrator is a pure coordinator (passes paths, reads the charter's `decomposition:` block), carries the anchor verification the mandate assigns it — dispatches the anchor reader whenever the Profiler requests (a mid-mandate halt, possibly multi-pass), and at the return point re-runs `tools/anchor-verify` against the charter's `anchor_verification` pin and checks the judged count against the passes' verdict artifacts together — and maintains the role roster (glossary: `role roster`): creates the roster file in the `cycle workspace` at the first dispatch and updates a row's state at every state change; at each return point it applies the role's output-contract parse rule (glossary: `output contract`) and marks the row.
3. **Profiler** — a fresh subagent (not the change-request author). Mandate — [`assets/decomposer-charter.md`](assets/decomposer-charter.md). Produces the charter (the `decomposition:` block per the mandate's Output contract): profile + multi-surface DSM + verdict. Dispatch/return — the two-branch completion rule (glossary: `dispatch-completion`; canon — [`../../norms/dispatch-completion.md`](../../norms/dispatch-completion.md)): a charter that parses into the Profiler's output contract completes the launch (never re-entered); a launch ending without one is completed by the same agent within the same frame.
4. Escalation — per the Escalation section: the halt is a wait; the author's answer arrives in the same launch's frame, the charter is revised to it over the profiled facts — the anchor rule's revision clause applying — and only then does the launch complete (a completed launch is never re-entered). Without the escalation the launch completes at item 3's return. A broken-launch report from the Profiler — the calibration or the metrics ledger missing or unreadable, a round-ledger missing where the prior cycle exists, the registry carrier's failed repair — halts the launch the same way: the input is repaired at its owner and the waiting run resumes with the input in place — the calibration and the ledger re-supplied into the same frame, the registry's carrier routed to `reconcile-change`, its repaired return resuming the run (a halt is a wait — glossary: `dispatch-completion`).

## Launch

After the pre-condition the orchestrator creates the `cycle workspace` — and, when R1 fired, dispatches the formation spike into it before the Profiler — and dispatches the Profiler with: the `cycle workspace` path and the charter's target path; the resolved baseline tree — its `product_root` (the pre-condition's resolution); the registry carrier path, resolved via the instance's carrier declaration; the instance's metrics ledger — its path, read-only; the prior cycle's round-ledger, when the change follows one (the AMPL input); the formation brief, when the R1 spike ran; the launch data — a slug + change request, or a re-decomposition context (which coupling was exposed, on which surface, by which skill, or which gate fix expands the change and what it adds) carrying the launch's slug, the prior charter's path, and the closed cycle's ledgers — and, on a launch through a red gate, the post-gate decision (which gate; the author's red-gate decision and its grounds — recorded by the Profiler in the charter's `gate_decision`); the project's calibration — its location (`docs/change-calibration.md`), its adoption gated by the pre-condition (the flag; the self-change checks the file itself); the standards-profile path, when the change runs over an external project clone that has one. At the return the orchestrator reads the charter from the file.
