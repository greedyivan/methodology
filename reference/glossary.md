# Glossary — change methodology

Term canon. Chain skills refer here; **terms are not redefined inline**. Living artifact: extended as new skills are introduced; editing existing terms goes through reconcile (rationale preserved in git history).

Canonical article keys are ASCII/EN, exactly one per article. Legacy RU keys were removed by the W3 latinization; a historical RU reference (pre-W3 revisions, frozen archives) resolves against the pre-W3 revision of this file.

## Instance topology

- **instance** — reference topology an adopting operator recreates: a root (not a git repo) with two git repos — the workspace-repo (method: the methodology clone — skills/, norms/, reference/, docs/; plus tools/, docs/; the .claude/ adapter) and the operational-repo (operational state: tasks/, memory/, telemetry/, registry/, meta/, projects/) — with project clones (`<root>/projects/`) and the root's harness adapter (`.claude/`). The base for relative paths of canons is the instance root.
- **cycle workspace** — the temporary space for one cycle's artifacts (`.claude/tmp/<cycle>/`, the root's harness adapter); valuables leave it via the retention pass at cycle close (classes commit/archive/prune; archive — .gz-in-git in operational-repo/meta/).

## Chain and artifacts

- **pre-request stage** — the author's work with an agent before a formal change request (problem exploration, context accumulation); its output is the change request + materials entering `scope-change`. Outside the change-cycle; a canon of lived practice.
- **change-cycle** — the sequence `scope-change → specify-change → audit-change → realize → verify-change → reconcile-change` applied to one change.
- **charter** — the output of `scope-change`: decomposition (single | program+DSM) + per-cycle profile/severity/blast-radius/reconcile-level.
- **spec** — the output of `specify-change`: five strata (intent, contract+frame, examples/properties, registry, realization-design-set) + fitness-map.
- **EARS** (Easy Approach to Requirements Syntax) — the canonical format of spec requirement lines (strata S1/S2): five templates in one list (condition-forward notation; keywords — the EN canon, placeholders — the spec's working language): Ubiquitous `the <system> shall <response>`; Event-driven `WHEN <trigger> the <system> shall <response>`; State-driven `WHILE <state> the <system> shall <response>`; Unwanted `IF <trigger>, then the <system> shall <response>`; Optional `WHERE <feature> is included, the <system> shall <response>`. Mandatory for full 5-strata (non-tool-authority), optional for light/emergent (tool-authoritative). Distinction from the G/W-T-scenario: EARS is the requirement format, G/W-T is the verification format of the same requirement; the WHEN/THEN structure closes the traceability triangle mechanically (requirement → test-scenario → doc-surface). Basis: Mavin et al., RE 2009, pp. 317–322, DOI 10.1109/re.2009.9.
- **frame** — the contract boundary: what a change **MAY** modify, what it **MUST** preserve.
- **registry** (decision-registry) — the Stratum-3 artifact: every fork/question/assumption/decision with classification.
- **impl-ledger** — the realize-decision journal of a cycle (`.claude/tmp/realize-change-<slug>/impl-ledger.md`): every fork/assumption adopted during realize gets a record at the moment of adoption (id RL-n bound to the spec registry's R-xx / classification / revision-condition + unit binding + realize-moment marker); the carrier of SENSOR lines of the realize stage. Protocol — realizer-charter of the `realize-change` skill; field canon — specifier-charter "Registry lifecycle in realize".
- **fitness** (fitness function) — an automated check guarding a contract invariant (atomic/holistic × cadence × static/dynamic × automated/manual).
- **SBCE** — Set-Based Concurrent Engineering: a set of admissible designs (≥2) with imposed distinct optimization objectives, comparison over fixed axes, a mandatory recommendation or hybrid; degeneration counterexample — "N variations of the first" (design fixation).
- **claim-list** — the program registry of falsifiable claims about artifacts/mechanics: canonical record fields `CL-<n> | claim | anchor | confidence` (one id-space within a program; anchor — `file:line` | repro-command | artifact-ref; a claim without an anchor is a record defect). Carrier `.claude/tmp/scope-change-<slug>/claim-list.md`, append-only within a program; not carried across programs (carrying is a promotion trigger). Facets — consumer projections over canonical fields, not schema fields: risk (Profiler: risk exposure + recurrence prior) / alternatives (Specifier: design option for Stratum-4) / anchors (Verifier: evidence for adjudication, read-only via tmp-dir) / feasibility (Realizer: unfamiliar mechanics); a new consumer projection does not change canonical fields. program-brief = claim-list + a topic table of contents (not a prose report, not a transcript). Boundary object: one schema consumed by the chain roles in their projections. Basis: Star–Griesemer 1989 (boundary object); Kitchenham 2004 (EBSE — evidence-based claims).
- **spike** — a timeboxed study on a pre-registered trigger; a pathway option (signpost + trigger + expiry), not a chain stage. Constant R1: pre-request code reading for understanding of ≥2 thematic blocks, or a retro assessment of ~100k input → a formation-spike with a budget ≤40k input-equivalent; output — the formation-brief (= program-brief: claim-list + topic TOC), amortized across all program cycles. The product of every spike — claims by the `claim-list` canonical line (the executor role's consumer projection), not a transcript and not a prose report. Triggers R2/R4 — at the carrier-executors (decomposer-charter, realizer-charter). Basis: Beck 1999 (spike, XP); Haasnoot 2013 (DAPP — dynamic adaptive policy pathways).
- **standards profile** — a linear checklist of standards for an external clone (6 sections: toolchain+versions; gates+Docker execution; repo structure; code conventions; commit conventions; where to look for task context), ≤60 lines including the header with the clone's HEAD pin; carrier `operational-repo/projects/<name>/standards.md`; maintained by the operator when taking a task over a clone without a current profile (by explicit command — a refresh recalculation); the profile path is injected into task prompts of cycles over the project instead of re-reconnaissance. Anchor: Pirolli–Card 1999 (information foraging) — a designed patch-structure of input; the volume ceiling guards against long-context degradation.

## Decisions and their classification

- **pressure-sensitive** — a decision that is irreversible (one-way door) **AND** has a low info-value of waiting → must be resolved before realize (it distorts if deferred under implementation pressure). Classification axes: *reversibility × info-value of waiting*.
- **LRM** (last responsible moment) — the point after which deferring a decision closes other options.
- **revision-condition** — an explicitly recorded condition for revisiting a decision: for a deferred one — "what info will close it + expiry"; for an accepted one (ADR) — "what trigger will force a reconsideration".
- **frontier** — the set of decisions whose premises are settled but whose answer is not closed by reading the codebase; in specify it produces a frontier-question package (each with a recommended answer) for the registry/checkpoint; the frontier recommends, the author decides at the checkpoint (non-intersection with MCDA decisions).

## Verification

- **fresh-context / author-independent** — verification by an agent that did not write the spec (adversarial, a mandate to refute).
- **silently-made** — a decision adopted in the spec or in realize without a registry record (the choice was made, not recognized as a fork).
- **silently-deferred** — a decision deferred past the LRM without a revision-condition.
- **scope-completeness** — the spec covers all real surfaces/artifacts/decision-sites of the change; completeness is assessed **against the theory of the declared type** (see "Scope by change type" below), not against the author's file list.
- **verdict** — the output of `audit-change`: `clean` ⇔ 0 BLOCKER ∧ 0 MAJOR by the `frozen checklist` ∧ every MINOR of the last findings has a `MINOR-disposition` record in the round-ledger (the `audit-cycle exit contract`); otherwise `blocked`.
- **test-oracle** — an independent source of an assertion's expected value (a literal / worked example / spec); an expected value computed by the same logic as the implementation is a tautological oracle (in verify-change consumption — the second axis of I-REF-semantic).
- **reconcile-level** (L0-L4) — tolerate / fix-realization / expand / minimal-revise-compatible / re-baseline-breaking.

## Scope by change type (type-theory-relative)

The completeness of a change's scope is assessed by the academic theory of its **declared type**, not by the author's file list. Type → the theory by which the Verifier (`audit-change`) judges completeness:

| Change type | Scope-completeness theory |
|---|---|
| dead-code-removal | **DCE fixpoint** — the transitive closure of deadness: everything unreachable from live roots, iterated to a fixpoint **across all artifacts** (code, docs, test inventory, configs, research notes) — any artifact referencing the removed thing enters the scope (becomes stale/broken); barrel/re-export is a special case of a code artifact |
| refactor | **behavior-preservation** — external behavior unchanged (Fowler) |
| contract-change | **LSP** / compatible-revision — weaken-pre / strengthen-post (Liskov-Wing) |
| feature-add | **requirements-completeness** (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148) |

**scope ≠ batch:** the fixpoint/theory defines the *logical* scope (what enters the change); commit granularity inside it is a separate batch decision (Reinertsen).

## Type-authority anchoring (resolving type-authority fragmentation)

Problem class: several legitimate definers of one runtime contract in a type system (owner types, the consumer's parallel universe, a peer-range of majors) have diverged structurally — the runtime is compatible, the types are not. The "anchoring" solution pattern: the consumer authoring-surface's types are derived from the **owner's protocol layer** (a stable type base; e.g. `@eslint/core`), resolved from the consumer's own dependencies (immunity to a major of the runtime dependency at the end consumer), consumer refinements — as an overlay, not a parallel world. Carve-out: the type guarantee for dead peer-range majors is dropped (the runtime guarantee remains). A universal type bridge across diverging definitions is practically absent (the intersection is empty or useless — variance analysis; do not look for one). Precedent carrier: a real anchoring cycle (discussion → R2-spike → contract). Attributions: Meyer DbC (the contract lives at the supplier); Pierce TAPL (structural subtyping/variance); the LSP precedent (one protocol vs N×M).

## Decomposition (multi-surface)

- **multi-surface DSM** — the Design Structure Matrix is built **across all affected surfaces** (code, docs, tests, configs), not code alone. Invariant: changes can be independent on the code-surface and coupled on the doc/test-surface → a coupled block. A DSM on the code-surface alone misses the coupling (found in real decomposition runs: code-disjoint, doc-coupled).
- **coupled block** — a set of changes coupled on some surface → resolved together (cannot be cleanly cut apart).
- **land-together** — the verdict for a surface conflict (code-disjoint, doc-coupled): separate cycles on code, but merged as one unit (master never sees intermediate stale states). **Viable only if every cycle is green-committable** — with a commit ban at a red gate (e.g. the project policy "no commit at red gate") an intermediate red cycle cannot be committed → land-together degenerates into a **merge** (one commit, when all are green). Realizes **scope ≠ batch**.

## Decomposition discipline (collapse gate)

- **decomposition-verdict** — the `scope-change` decision on the work structure along three **granularity axes**: work-unit (units of specification/work) / verify-unit (units of verification) / land-unit (units of merge) — **independent decisions**; deriving one axis from another (in particular work from land semantics, including the land-together→merge degeneration) is an anti-pattern. Checked by the `collapse-checklist`.
- **collapse-checklist** — the named checklist in `decomposer-charter.md` (AXES / COORD / DESRULE / COST / AMPL / GATE); a collapse verdict (any collapsing of work granularity) is not issued without a recorded per-item pass. Binding the rules to the decision point counters the knowing-doing gap.
- **coordination mechanism by coupling type** — a dependency is resolved by a mechanism, not by merging: weak coupling through a shared layer → an aggregate integration pass over the layer; shared-resource → ownership/protocol; prerequisite → sequencing. A full merge of units — only when the units are not separately feasible, justified through the `verification-cost-model`.
- **design-rules-first** — the common layer (term set, conventions, checklist skeleton) is frozen before work branches into units; "one common pass at the end" is an anti-pattern (late binding of the common interface).
- **verification-cost-model** — with any collapse verdict, a verification-cost estimate is mandatory: the consistency surface = the pairwise joints of the parts (O(k²) in the number of parts k within one spec) + the expected audit rounds; an authoring-only estimate is separate and insufficient.
- **amplitude control bounds** — the audit cycle's round-ledger (round, counters by severity, finding class; carrier — the `audit-change` §Cycle) + bounds: **M** = ≥2 same-class findings across cycles → mandatory RCA; **N** = the first repeat round without a change of finding class after an applied RCA → stop + the authoring question "re-decompose?" (not an auto-gate; the author's decision is recorded in the ledger). Process control, not product acceptance.

## Exit contract of the audit cycle (product acceptance)

- **audit-cycle exit contract** — the acceptance predicate for the `verdict` (see `verdict`): cleanliness = no BLOCKER/MAJOR by the frozen checklist + all MINORs with disposition. Carriers: the `audit-change/SKILL.md` §Cycle (the predicate, executor — the orchestrator: an algorithmic reconciliation of findings counters ↔ disposition records of the round-ledger) + `verifier-charter` (checking against the checklist, disposition recommendations). Attributions — the Attributions section of `verifier-charter`.
- **frozen checklist** — a versioned instancia of the verification standard in the cycle's tmp-dir (4 categories × dimensions + basic grep checks); derived from the canon (glossary + verifier-charter) at cycle start (a computed instancia, not a hand-declared list); exists before round 1 — absence = stop. Monotone within a cycle: an observation on a dimension outside the checklist is a **process finding** (→ round-ledger → reconcile, enters the next cycle's checklist; the author may by explicit decision promote it to blocking — recorded in the ledger). Attributions — the Attributions section of `verifier-charter`.
- **MINOR-disposition** — the author's decision on a MINOR finding (on the Verifier's recommendation with evidence): **fix-now** (a point fix + scoped re-inspection; the disposition closes with a green outcome) | **defer** (a registry record with a revision-condition). Recording — a disposition record in the round-ledger (finding-id + decision + rationale); the findings header is a momentary snapshot of the round, not overwritten. Attributions — the Attributions section of `verifier-charter`.
- **re-inspection-scope** — after a MINOR fix-now fix, the changed fragments + their immediate consumption-sites are rechecked, not the whole corpus; a full pass — after blocker/major fixes or by the author's explicit decision. The verify-stage analog — `read-reinspect` (closing an L1 fix). Attributions — the Attributions section of `verifier-charter`.
- **process-control ≠ product-acceptance** — the separation of the cycle's two mechanics: `amplitude control bounds` govern the process (stop + authoring question), the `audit-cycle exit contract` accepts the product (a verdict by the frozen checklist); conflation in edited texts is forbidden. Attributions — the Attributions section of `verifier-charter`.

## Skill authoring

- **llm-consumed-invariants** — artifact `norms/llm-consumed-invariants.md` (former name: skill-authoring-invariants): an authoring-invariants checklist for the class of LLM-consumed surfaces (class inventory — No. 6 of the norm) + the resync rule.
- **resync-trigger** — the same-changeset synchronization rule: a skill behavior change ⇒ a README delta (the skill's line) and a glossary delta (if terms) in the same changeset; anti-pattern — "the router that lies".
- **LLM-consumed** — a property of a text surface: its instructions are consumed by LLM sessions as guidance (they govern session behavior), as opposed to data that a session reads as descriptions (registries, inventories, docs prose, markup templates). The instruction-vs-data boundary for a borderline file is decided by a content run: a file read by a session as an instruction — in the class. The inventory of class surfaces — No. 6 in `norms/llm-consumed-invariants.md` (an inventory snapshot of git ls-files; class extension — re-decomposition, not an in-place edit of the inventory). A behavioral edit of an LLM-consumed surface requires eval-first evidence: the changeset carries a run of a representative task before/after the edit (the categorical criterion — routing, task uptake, gates, output formats, stopping conditions, glossary term rules).

## Checkpoints and decisions

- **checkpoint** — a mandatory pause (emit-and-halt) on a significant scope/method shift; the request author = ground truth (intent/values), the agent verifies, does not substitute. There are no optional checkpoints.
- **MCDA / Value-Focused Thinking** — the decision method at a checkpoint (Keeney; Keeney-Raiffa, *Decisions with Multiple Objectives*): **criteria** (from intent) first → alternatives scored against the criteria → author **value-weights** → the decision follows from criteria × weights. Not preference-based, not "think for me". The agent's role — alternatives × criteria × evidence; the author's role — value-weights.
- **authoring-gate (scope/realization change)** — a scope/realization decision (direction, method choice, realization change, **deviation from the plan or a methodology default**) MANDATORILY goes through authoring: **explicit notification of the author about the change + confirmation of the new path**; the process does **STOP + WAIT**. The author = the carrier of product context; their input can **override a methodology-obvious choice** (the B3 canon: methodology chose the "obvious" A, the author's context [null→from = trap] turned it to B). **Autonomy (without the author) — ONLY for track/routing/sequencing/necessity; NOT for scope/realization decisions.** Anti-pattern: declaring a scope/realization decision "process-determined" and skipping authoring (the author's context not requested → an erroneous autonomous choice).
- **decision-defense** — when the author overrides a methodology default: the author provides product context + a justification of the override. The mechanism by which the author's context turns the "obvious by methodology" choice. The process stops and waits for the author's input/decision.

## Fitness and causal analysis

- **fitness-authority-by-capability** — the authority of checking follows the instrumental capability: **tooling-authoritative** where a tool exists (knip/lint/typecheck/doc-links — authoritative, ecosystem-maintained); **human-authoritative** where no tool exists (doc-prose links — human review); grep/self-computed — a backstop only. Do not hand-roll what tools already do (reinvention = recurrent findings every audit cycle). Not "bar-lowering" but "the right authority per check".
- **computed fitness (fallback)** — where no ready tool exists, fitness derives the target-set from artifacts at check time (not a hand-declared static list — hand-declaration is fundamentally incomplete).
- **causal analysis in the audit loop** — a recurrent finding class (≥2 same-class across cycles) → **mandatory RCA** (CMMI CAR; Chillarege ODC: a cluster of same-class defects = the signature of a weak process stage) → eliminate the root cause → adversarial re-audit. Not patch-the-instance. **Non-convergence = the signal that the RCA is insufficient** (the symptoms were treated).

## Skill-gap closure (chain self-extension)

- **skill-gap closure** — when a chain step references a skill that does not exist (the chain is built refine-in-use):
  1. **DETECT** — the chain reached a step whose skill is missing.
  2. **STOP** — no silent fallback, no ad-hoc handler; the process halts.
  3. **BUILD** — build the skill **from a real case** (not speculatively): the real case = practice input; theory = the glossary; the pattern = existing skills (SKILL.md + assets/charter + fresh-context subagents).
  4. **VERIFY** — verify the skill **by processing a real case with it** (verification by construction).
  5. **CONTINUE** — the chain proceeds with the new skill.

  **Anti-pattern:** building a skill without a real trigger (speculatively) = a dry-run artifact. **Invariant:** every skill of the chain is built from a real case and verified by it.

## Gate integrity (green baseline)

- **green-baseline (entry pre-condition)** — a change starts from a state where the gates pass. Before starting edits — explicitly check the gates **by running them**; if red → **preliminary greening**: a separate prerequisite-change fixes the gate BEFORE the main change. Basis: CI keep-the-build-green (Fowler; Duvall-Matyas-Glover, *Continuous Integration*); start-from-known-good. An entry precondition for `scope-change`.
- **no-commit-on-red** — committing is forbidden at any red gate. Enforced **tooling-authoritatively**: a pre-commit hook (`scripts/hooks/pre-commit` + `git config core.hooksPath`) runs the full CI target (all suites) and blocks the commit at red. Development proceeds **without commits** (edits accumulate); small gates (lint/typecheck/test/knip) — ad hoc for feedback; the commit — only when the full `make ci` is green (~once per cycle, rarely). ≡ fitness-authority-by-capability (tooling where a tool exists, not human discipline — discipline has already failed: the author committed at a red gate). (Already implied in land-together ↔ merge.)
- **gate-closing fast-track** — a gate red DURING/after the work for a reason **independent of the edits** (a security advisory, a surfaced pre-existing debt, env drift) → the fix enters the current work **fast-track**, to **close** the gate (correct — not bypass, not defer, not `--no-verify`). ≡ reconcile **L2 expand** (the change extends to include the gate fix); a change does not land on a red gate. Basis: stop-the-line / Jidoka-Andon (do not pass a defect through); fix-forward; definition-of-done (gates-green). Anti-pattern: demote/bypass (≡ bar-lowering, see fitness-authority-by-capability).
- **Docker boundary of execution** (materialized by reconcile from a real incident series): the Docker-only canon covers execution of target-project/lab code (go build/test/bench/fuzz, gate scripts); host-side parsing of ready artifacts by a validator (awk/diff/benchstat output) — outside the canon: project code is not executed, artifact files are read.

## Process mode: accumulate-then-verify (fitness authority determines depth)

- **fitness-authority determines process-depth** — the depth of the specify/audit/verify chain is calibrated by `fitness-authority-by-capability`. The mode's authorship — `scope-change` (the profile determines which tool is authoritative for a given change). **All chain skills inherit the mode** (the glossary is authoritative; do not redefine inline).
- **accumulate-then-verify (tool-authoritative changes):** for changes whose correctness is checked by an authoritative tool (dead-code→knip, types→typecheck, behavior→tests), the tool = verify. Mode: **edit directly (no-commit, rollback is cheap) → run the tool → feedback → iterate**. spec = **light frame** (intent / scope-in / scope-out / MUST-preserve / fitness-tools), NOT a line-level realization-design-set; audit — **light/skip** (the tool verifies realize); verify = the tool on the actual edits (not vs an "ideal spec"). **= Tier-1** of two-tier verification (see below); `verify-change` = **Tier-2** sparse semantic checkpoint.
- **describe-then-apply (heavy specify/audit) — reserved** for changes **without direct tool authority**: behavioral (no deterministic test), design forks, contractual, high-risk. Here spec = contract (invariants/examples/registry), audit = adversarial pre-realize.
- **edit→validation-chain preference (dry-run = last resort):** where an edit→validation chain exists (tool: typecheck / phpunit / knip / contract-drift / doc-links / traceability), **USE it** (live edit + tool feedback) — NOT a dry run. A dry run (spec-verify/audit WITHOUT edits) is permissible **ONLY when the chain does NOT exist** (semantic, no tool). **Absence of a chain = a signal**: tooling should be extended to create one (make semantic tool-verifiable). Anti-pattern: dry-run-audit/reasoning about a typecheck break that typecheck would have caught directly by edit+run. Canon: role-cascade — typecheck caught address-suggest LSP-break instantly (ground truth); a dry-run audit would have found the same thing slowly/incompletely/at higher cost.
- Basis: `fitness-authority-by-capability` + severity calibration (the Tiny fast path is a special case). **A dry-run spec/audit for a tool-verifiable change = over-engineering** and breeds its own bugs (artifacts of description-without-action: "keep export {X}", an inoperative grep pattern). Anti-pattern: hand-rolling (grep/spec) what a tool already checks; preparing "an ideal description to apply later" instead of "accumulate edits → verify".

## Necessity filter (do/don't-do gate)

- **necessity filter** — a per-cycle assessment at `scope-change` AFTER decomposition (verdict → every allocated cycle is filtered). It applies the calibration to each cycle: does it serve a **HIGH-priority quality attribute** (③) OR resolve **acknowledged tech debt** (②.2) OR is it required by gate-closing; is it **stage-appropriate** (①.4: acceptance → behavior-preserving OK, long-term-investment questionable); is it **not over-engineered** (minimally sufficient; abstract-reference-parity is not the measure);
- **Outcome per cycle:** **DO** (serves a concrete need) | **DEFER** (long-term-investment on an unstable stage; with a revision-condition; a record in the deferred-options registry — `deferred-option`) | **DROP** (speculative / serves no need / over-engineered; with rationale, does not enter the registry). DEFER/DROP cycles are excluded from the program; the charter records the rationale.
- **Especially important under program decomposition:** scope-change may decompose a request into N cycles; the necessity filter checks each — some may turn out unneeded (DROP) and must not enter the work. The input filter "do/don't do", applied BEFORE specify/realize.
- **Methodology-derived, not a user menu:** the outcome is derived from the calibration (see `the process determines the track` / `the reference standard dictates — autonomy`); the change author is the ground truth for intent, but the scope/necessity of each cycle is assessed by the process. Complementary to audit (mid-process) and verify (post): necessity = the input gate.

## Legitimacy invariants and the shadow phase

- **legitimacy invariants** — a closed list of 5 invariants (L1: checklist freeze / L2: authoring-gate / L3: exit contract / L4: baseline immutability + revert / L5: anytime form), provably preserved by every self-change cycle (the proof obligation — the invariant table L(i)×evidence); product cycles are under a vacuum. Canon: `norms/legitimacy-invariants.md`.
- **shadow phase** — self-application of a process improvement before land in instrumented mode (the baseline untouched until acceptance); emission of the SHADOW delta of GQM metrics (the marker line of the metrics ledger; the `revert-guard` signal); acceptance — an authoring gate, not an auto-gate. Canon: legitimacy-invariants.md.

## Process monitor

- **monitor summary** — a computable aggregate of the M1–M8 metrics over the formal-era window (round-ledgers + SENSOR lines + canon sources; the procedure and the closed set — `norms/monitor.md`): formative, does not gate; signals against pre-registered norms (calibration §⑨); a signal firing → an authoring question (the R4 canon). Executed by the orchestrator at the LAND-point of the verify-change Checkpoint.

## Sensor layer (deviation/near-miss)

- **sensor line** — the unified sensor-record format of a chain executor: one structured line with the grep marker `SENSOR`, written at the moment of the event (not retro prose; post-factum — only with an explicit value of the recording point) into an existing artifact of its stage (round-ledger / charter / spec-registry / verdict artifact / impl-ledger; no new files are created), a self-report about one's own deviation (the ESM canon; an observation about someone else's behavior — a process finding, the channels are independent, absorption forbidden). Formative — outside verdict counters, the exit predicate, M/N amplitude and the severity mechanics. Format: `SENSOR | kind=<deviation|near-miss> | actor=<role> | stage=<scope|specify|audit|realize|verify|reconcile> | outcome=<bypass-succeeded|bypass-failed|averted> | point=<in-moment|post-factum> | <ostensive fact> :: <performative expectation>`. The enumerations are closed (grep-exhaustible): kind ∈ {deviation — a performative↔ostensive divergence, the outcome "the bypass succeeded/failed"; near-miss — the weak signal "X almost happened", the outcome averted}; stage — the 6 chain stages; outcome ∈ {bypass-succeeded, bypass-failed, averted}; point ∈ {in-moment, post-factum}. Any line with the marker and an incomplete field set is a format defect; an event without the marker is not a sensor line. Field extension (the k12 class of reconcile records, Monitor fields) is allowed without changing the marker or the existing fields. Basis: Feldman–Pentland 2003, *AMR* (performative/ostensive); Csikszentmihalyi–Larson 1987 (Experience Sampling Method — capturing at the moment of the event); Weick–Sutcliffe, *Managing the Unexpected* (HRO — the near-miss as a weak signal).

## Registry of deferred options

- **deferred-option** — an option deferred by the necessity filter: a real mechanic with an operational trigger predicate, a full anchor set and a build path; lives in the registry `operational-repo/registry/deferred-options.md`; DROP records do not enter the registry.
- **trigger predicate** — the operational firing condition of a deferred option: an Event class (checkpoint question / audit finding / verify adjudication / profiling fact) + a decidable predicate over an observation of that class; the matching yields an outcome from {fired, not fired}, no third outcome.
- **trigger-matching point** — a chain-skill step matching run observations against the trigger predicates of the deferred-options registry (an ECA hook: event → condition → action per the firing canon — stop + an authoring question, not an auto-gate).
- **expiry** — the obsolescence condition of a deferred option; at the registry's revision point (`reconcile-change`) expired items are dropped or re-derived; the registry does not grow without revision.

## Instance-state (the typed-anchor convention)

- **instance-state** — the class of a consumer's project artifacts living outside the corpus's portable content, at a typed path in the operational repo (a typed anchor: the path resolves deterministically from the instance root without project context). Carrier instance: `operational-repo/telemetry/metrics-ledger.md` (the Monitor's formal-era window list). Contrast: the corpus's normative content is portable and replicated by cloning; instance-state artifacts belong to the consuming instance and do not enter the corpus. Basis: precedent 0601192c (doc-conformance-registry moved out of the corpus into instance-state); Brinch Hansen 1970 (mechanism/policy — the portable corpus as mechanism, instantiation as policy).

## Instance-spec (the instance surfaces inventory)

- **instance-spec** — a living registry of the instance's canonical surfaces ("the system's table of contents"): lines "name → purpose in one line → path from the instance root", sections per repo (workspace-repo / operational-repo / the root adapter); carrier — `workspace-repo/canon/instance-spec.md`. Carrier separation: the instance's AGENTS.md canon — rules and processes, instance-spec — the inventory of "what exists" facts; the AGENTS.md topology map — the frozen path schema, instance-spec — a living registry. Updated by deltas of the classes ADDED/MODIFIED/REMOVED by the name-status of the closing cycle's changeset, applied by reconcile-change intra-changeset (a body edit before land; the journal — git history). Distinction from **instance-state**: instance-state — the typed-anchor convention for the consumer's project artifacts (the placement mechanism outside the corpus), instance-spec — the instance's own registry-table of contents. Basis: Bohner–Arnold 1996 (eds.), *Software Change Impact Analysis* (IEEE CS Press — impact analysis: an edit → the derived surfaces of the registry); the living-spec-with-deltas genre precedent — OpenSpec (openspec.dev).

## Traceability triangle (change closing: behavior↔test↔doc)

- **traceability triangle** — a single traceability key ties **behavior ↔ test ↔ doc**, closing the change loop within one artifact (BDD / Specification-by-Example — Adzic; RTM; DoD "tested + documented"). A behavioral change: key(s) → **test-scenarios** (examples, executable — TDD) → **doc-surfaces** (where the behavior is described). All three are identified in the spec (`specify-change` S2), checked in `verify-change` (I-TRACE). The project instantiates the key (calibration).
- **Depth by change type:** behavior-adding/modifying (feature-add, behavior-modifying refactor, contract-change) — the triangle is **mandatory** (a new/changed key → test + doc). Behavior-preserving (a refactor without a behavior change, dead-code-removal) — the existing key(s) tests stay green; the docs stay accurate (lighter).
- **tests guard, are not merely executed:** coverage (execution) is weak; **mutation testing** (a test fails if behavior is wrong — Stryker/PIT) — a strong signal, for **high-risk** changes. Docs living: the doc-surfaces of the affected behavior are updated in the changeset (Diátaxis + docs-as-code; Bohner-Arnold impact analysis: the blast radius determines the affected tests/docs).

## Two-tier verification (continuous Tier-1 + sparse Tier-2; split by fitness authority)

Cheap live edits + fast authoritative gates make the **closed loop** (edit→gate→edit) practical — it replaces the batch Fagan cycle (the expensive edits of the past). But author bias (the Fagan independence principle: the author systematically does not see their own errors) does not vanish at the semantic layer. → **two-tier verification**, split by `fitness-authority-by-capability`:

- **Tier-1 — continuous closed-loop (author + tool):** edit → authoritative gate (test/typecheck/knip/traceability/doc-links) → feedback → edit, until convergence. The tool = an **independent authority** (not the author) → author bias is absent → convergence suffices. The bulk of edits here (mechanical + behavior-under-test). **Replaces the cycled-batch** `validate-implementation`/`validate-docs` — both skills removed from the repo (the Fagan cycle is obsolete with cheap edits — their function is absorbed by this loop + Tier-2).
- **Tier-2 — sparse independent checkpoint (fresh-context):** an adversarial agent ≠ the author, at key points, **semantic only** (where the tool is absent): assertion guards (a test *guards* behavior, is not tagged), doc factual truth, design appropriateness, contracts. **Not a cycle** — a one-off/rare checkpoint (pre-commit for semantic risk, handover). Fagan independence preserved, but **sparser** (Tier-1 took the volume).
- **The split criterion = fitness authority:** tool-verifiable → Tier-1 (continuous, tool=authority); semantic (no tool, author bias) → Tier-2 (sparse-independent). Triangle co-evolution (code+test+doc together) = living-doc/SbE, evolves in the Tier-1 loop.
- **Grounding:** closed-loop control / cybernetics (Wiener); TDD red-green (Beck); SbE/living-doc (Adzic); Fagan inspection (independence — sparse, not batched); continuous flow (Reinertsen); exploratory/spikes (XP). Cycled-batch (`validate-impl`/`validate-docs`) — **superseded**, the skills removed from the repo.
- **Process architecture:** scope → light/emergent spec → research edits + Tier-1 continuous (triangle co-evolution; rollback/stop natural) → converged realization (Tier-1 green) → **Tier-2 sparse semantic checkpoint** (`verify-change`) → commit. `specify` heavy 5-strata + `audit` pre-realize — only for non-tool-authority (design/contract/high-risk), where research edits do not suffice.

## Authority distribution

- **terminal authority** — the terminal (acceptance) decision of the process is made by a tool (an authoritative gate within its capability — `fitness-authority-by-capability`) or by the author (an `authoring-gate`); an LLM agent is an executor and/or recommender, not a terminal authority. An agent's adjudication/findings — a classification for routing (L0/L1 are closed by Tier-1 gates/a record; L2+ — authoring), not acceptance. Distinction: `fitness-authority-by-capability` — the authority *of checking* (who verifies: tool vs human); terminal authority — the authority *of acceptance* of the process (who makes the terminal decision: an authoritative gate vs the author); an LLM agent — executor/recommender on both axes. Basis: Parasuraman–Sheridan–Wickens 2000 (decision-authority distribution); Horvitz 1999 (mixed-initiative).

## read-reinspect (verify stage, closing L1)

- **read-reinspect** — the closing step of a verify cycle: every applied L1 fix (adjudication L1 fix-realization) is closed by a fresh reader (a subagent ≠ the realizer ≠ the Verifier who issued the adjudication), who re-reads the applied fixes + their immediate consumption-sites; the outcome (confirmation/mismatch) is recorded in the verdict artifact. Mandatory at ≥1 applied L1 fix of any application loop — including the orchestrator's fixes in a run combining the Tier-1 loop and the Tier-2 checkpoint; a cycle without L1 fixes produces no step. Not a second verify run: a scoped recheck of the changed within a single checkpoint. Carrier — verify-change/SKILL.md (Checkpoint p.5). Distinction: `re-inspection-scope` — the audit stage, closing MINOR fix-now (the same scoped-recheck mechanics at another chain stage). Attributions: Fagan 1976, *IBM Syst. J.* (re-inspection/follow-up on the changed); practice source — real verify cycles (metrics-ledger window).
